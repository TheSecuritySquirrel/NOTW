Surveillance & Policing

Nevada Police May Be Tracking Your Phone's Location Without a Warrant

  • Nevada police partnered with a data broker called Fog Data Science to access near real-time cellphone location data — no warrant required.
  • The data comes from everyday mobile apps, allowing authorities to map individuals' movements and daily patterns.
  • This process sidesteps Fourth Amendment protections and operates with little transparency or oversight.
Cybersecurity

"Addicted to Hacking"

  • A new generation of young, highly skilled hackers is driving major data breaches and posing growing security risks to businesses and individuals.
  • One hacker described being "addicted" to hacking, progressing from youthful curiosity to serious criminal activity.
  • The path ultimately led to arrest and prison — a stark reminder of the real legal and personal consequences.
AI & Data Privacy

Shuttered Startups Are Selling Old Slack Chats and Emails to AI Companies

  • When startups go bankrupt, their internal data — including Slack messages and emails — can become a sellable asset to AI training companies.
  • Most employees never know their private work communications may be repurposed after a company closes.
  • Experts raise concerns about consent, exposure of sensitive business information, and the near-total lack of regulation.
Legislation & Policy

Virginia Bans Sale of Geolocation Data

  • Virginia has passed a law banning the sale of individuals' geolocation data without their explicit consent.
  • The law targets data brokers and apps that collect and monetize sensitive location information.
  • It reflects a growing wave of state-level privacy regulation as concerns about digital surveillance continue to rise.
